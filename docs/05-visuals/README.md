## The interpretation rule

A picture without a written interpretation proves nothing. Every artifact above needs a
paragraph answering: **what am I looking at, and what does it tell me the model has learned?**

For the attention heatmaps specifically, name what each head appears to do — attends to the
previous character, attends to spaces or line breaks, spreads attention evenly, focuses on the
start of the sequence. Being wrong here is fine; not looking is not.

## How to render a heatmap without a plotting library

Map each value to a character by magnitude, e.g. `" .:-=+*#%@"`, and print row by row with the
axes labelled. For SVG, emit `<rect>` elements with a computed fill — plain string formatting,
no dependencies. Document whichever scale you choose, and whether it is linear or logarithmic;
an undocumented colour scale makes a heatmap unreadable.

# Interpretations, experiment 001

## 10 — Loss curve

```
  5.02 | t
  4.54 | v
  3.73 | t t
  3.40 | v vt v
  2.92 |       tttv  v     v
  2.59 |           vttt vvt tvtvt  t    v t   v  t  v   tt t     t      v
  2.43 |             tvt   t t tvttv ttvtt  ttt tvtvttttv vt  vtvv  vttt tt vt
  2.27 |                t        v  t   tvvv   t   ttv v vtvttt ttvtt  tt vttvt
  1.94 |                                                           v
       +----------------------------------------------------------------------
        1                                                                 2000
```

Three phases, and they line up with the three things the model can learn in this order:

1. **Steps 1-100, a cliff from 5.02 to about 3.4.** This is not the model getting clever, it's
   the model learning that the characters have different base rates. Space is 15.23% of the text
   and 'e' is 8.48%, so just guessing in proportion already beats guessing uniformly. That's why
   this drop is so fast and so cheap.
2. **Steps 100-600, down to about 2.5.** Now it's using the previous character or two. Which
   letters follow which, that 'q' is followed by 'u', that a capital letter follows a newline.
3. **Steps 600-2000, a slow grind from 2.5 to 2.2 with a lot of noise.** This is where real
   spelling would have to be learned, and 40k parameters with a 32 character context is not much
   to do it with. It's still improving, just slowly.

The train line is noisy because each point is a single batch of 4 sequences, so it's one small
sample rather than a measurement of the whole corpus. Validation loss sits at or below train loss
throughout, which means the model is nowhere near overfitting. It's underfitting: too small for
the data, not too big. So the useful next move is a bigger model or a longer run, not
regularisation.

## 08 — Attention heads

Each grid is one head, cell `(i, j)` = how much position `i` attended to position `j`, shaded
against each row's own maximum. Blank above the diagonal is the mask, exactly zero.

**Block 0, head 0 — the "previous character" head.** Almost everything sits on the diagonal and
the cell just left of it:

```
        F i r s t _ C i t i z e n :
t      |      ++@@
_      |        --@@
C      |          @@++
i      |            @@
t      |          ....@@##
```

Position `t` looks at itself and a little at `s` before it. This is a local, bigram-style head,
and it's exactly what a model needs first: what character just happened.

**Block 0, head 1 — wider, and it holds onto a few earlier positions.** More shading spread
across the row, several columns lit at once rather than just the diagonal.

**Block 1, head 1 — it goes back to structural anchors, not to neighbours.** This is the most
interesting one. Look at the column it keeps returning to:

```
:      |++                        @@
\n     |..                        @@
B      |  ..    ..@@::......
w      |          %%                              @@..
_      |          ##  ::                          @@    ##
g      |          **  ..                          @@    %%
```

Several positions late in the sentence attend strongly back to column 10, which is the `z` of
"Citizen", and to the start of the line, rather than to whatever is immediately before them. So
the second block is not repeating the first block's job. It's using the space that block 0's local
head freed up to look at where it is in the line, which is what produces the `NAME:` newline
structure in the samples.

That progression, local first then structural, is why 2 layers do better than 1 here.

## 09 — Embedding neighbours

Cosine similarity between rows of the embedding table. Nothing in the code groups characters. Any
structure here was learned only from which characters turned out to be useful in the same places.

```
- `.` -> ? 0.96, ! 0.92, ; 0.73, 3 0.67, : 0.64, , 0.62
- `t` -> T 0.66, w 0.35, W 0.35, s 0.33, g 0.33, y 0.32
- `a` -> i 0.59, A 0.55, u 0.51, O 0.50, o 0.44, b 0.38
- `o` -> O 0.50, q 0.47, i 0.45, a 0.44, I 0.34, j 0.29
- `q` -> Q 0.55, B 0.53, J 0.53, o 0.47, g 0.43, X 0.41
- `_` -> - 0.75, & 0.52, \n 0.46, X 0.45, A 0.34, i 0.29
- `\n` -> - 0.57, & 0.54, _ 0.46, U 0.44, E 0.39, ' 0.31
```

Three real findings:

1. **Sentence-ending punctuation has collapsed into one cluster.** `.` and `?` are at 0.96 and
   `!` at 0.92. That is nearly the same vector. Which makes sense: for predicting what comes
   next, `.`, `?` and `!` are interchangeable, all three are followed by a space or a newline and
   then a capital. The model has discovered they're the same kind of thing without being told.
2. **Upper and lower case versions of the same letter pair up.** `t`/`T` at 0.66, `a`/`A` at 0.55,
   `o`/`O` at 0.50, `q`/`Q` at 0.55. Nothing in the tokenizer connects them, they're just ids 45
   and 32 or whatever. The only reason they're close is that they behave the same way in text.
3. **Whitespace groups with whitespace.** Space and `\n` at 0.46, and both are near `-`.

`a`'s neighbours are `i`, `u`, `o`, so vowels are grouping a bit too, but weakly. That's the kind
of thing that would sharpen with a bigger `d_model`, more steps, or both.

The honest caveat: 32 dimensions and 2000 steps is not much, so some of these numbers are partly
noise. `z -> v 0.80` and `q -> B 0.53` are hard to justify and are probably just rare characters
that never got enough gradient to be pushed anywhere meaningful. Rare characters staying random is
itself the expected result, and it's worth saying so rather than reading a story into it.
